01 / Team Hyperion's Mission Accessibility First

Making lunar survival intuitive through Solaris

Traditional space agency documents present lunar South Pole geology, thermodynamic phase changes, and space weather hazards as dense technical reports. Unless you already speak aerospace engineering, the reality of how astronauts survive on the Moon remains locked behind complex scientific jargon.

Our mission with Solaris is to make cryo-drilling at Shackleton Crater, vacuum water purification, and low-angle solar array alignment feel immediately tangible—so any student or curious explorer can experience how NASA navigates the extreme challenges of lunar exploration.

02 / Why We Built Solaris Science + Play

Bridging complex space science and interactive education for young explorers

During Team Hyperion's brainstorming session, we noticed that children either read dry static articles about lunar missions like Artemis or play sci-fi games that ignore real space physics altogether. Young learners miss out on understanding the actual challenges of surviving harsh cosmic environments.

We designed Solaris as an educational platform where children can explore how to handle extreme conditions—from managing vacuum thermodynamics to drilling ice in Shackleton Crater—through engaging, hands-on simulation gameplay that teaches them real space survival.

Team Hyperion • The Challenge Statement

Challenges we took on &
technical hurdles overcome

Team Hyperion tackled the "Build a Junior Astronaut Mission Trainer"challenge track—transforming complex aerospace engineering and harsh-condition survival mechanics into the cohesive, accessible Solaris educational experience designed for young learners.

01

Shackleton Crater Cryo-Stratigraphy

Translating complex planetary geology into intuitive puzzle mechanics, allowing children to safely extract ice core samples by tuning drill bit frequencies and managing equipment wear against abrasive regolith.

02

Modular Mini-Game Architecture

Architecting a clean, decoupled JavaScript and CSS structure (game.js, drill-game.js, water-game.js, solar-game.js) to seamlessly manage multiple interactive simulation panes without crashing or losing resource states.

03

Zero-Dependency Browser Sim

Ensuring students on low-bandwidth classroom laptops or tablets can launch Solaris instantly without gigabyte downloads, logins, or heavy GPU requirements.

Team Hyperion Mission Log

How Team Hyperion built Solaris in 48 hours

Hour 01 — 08

Challenge & Data Curation

Selected our NASA Space Apps challenge statement ("Build a Junior Astronaut Mission Trainer"), audited lunar South Pole datasets (Shackleton Crater conditions, regolith composition, and solar vectors), and sketched the educational UI layout..

Hour 09 — 20

Telemetry & Mini-Game Design

Authored the core logic for our interactive educational subsystems—mapping cryo-stratigraphy drilling, thermodynamic water purification, and low-angle solar array alignment into kid-friendly browser puzzles.

Hour 21 — 36

JavaScript & UI Integration

Coded the HTML5/CSS3 Mission Control dashboard and integrated decoupled JavaScript game modules (drill-game.js, water-game.js, solar-game.js) with real-time resource and telemetry sync.

Hour 37 — 48

Polish & Final Launch

Conducted cross-device browser testing, refined the glassmorphism UI styling, added success animations and audio cues, and finalized the Solaric demo for NASA Space Apps.
